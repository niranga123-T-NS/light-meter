TALQ smart street lighting

Follow the messages between the lights, the network, the management software and other city systems
1

In one line

TALQ is an open standard from the TALQ Consortium for the interface between a Central Management Software (CMS) and the outdoor device networks (gateways, luminaire controllers) – so a city can use one CMS with street-light controllers from different makers.
2

See it – press a scenario

3

The layers

LuminaireLED driver – ideally D4i (DALI-2 for IoT) so it reports energy, hours, temperature and failures
Luminaire controller (node)Plugs into a Zhaga or NEMA socket on the luminaire; switches, dims, measures, may carry sensors
Outdoor networkRF mesh, LoRaWAN, NB-IoT / LTE-M or cellular – depends on the vendor
GatewayRuns schedules locally and speaks TALQ to the CMS
Direct-to-CMS nodes (no gateway)Many CMS makers’ own controllers use cellular NB-IoT / LTE-M and talk straight to their CMS in the maker’s own protocol. A third-party maker’s lights join that CMS through their own TALQ-certified gateway.
TALQ interfaceStandard web-service messages (HTTPS) for configuration, commands, calendars & control programs, data collection, events & alarms
CMSThe city’s single map and dashboard: assets, schedules, energy, failures, reports
Third-party systemsGIS / asset register, maintenance work orders, energy billing, smart-city platform, traffic / weather / air-quality sensors – connected through the CMS’s own APIs (TALQ 2 also covers other smart-city devices)
4

Say this to the client

“With a TALQ-certified CMS and gateways, your system is not tied to one brand – you can add other makers’ lights later and still manage everything from one map, and send failures straight to your maintenance system.”
5

Watch out

Ask for TALQ certification of both the CMS and the gateway (the TALQ Consortium lists certified products) – “TALQ compatible” on a brochure is not the same. Agree early who owns the data and the CMS licence, and which third-party interfaces (work orders, GIS, billing) are in the price.

Series circuit – from the vault to the light

Tap each part: regulator, primary cable, connectors, isolating transformer, secondary cable, light base, monitoring module
1

In one line

The regulator in the vault sends 6.6 A round a primary cable loop of several kilometres. At every light the loop passes through an isolating transformer in the light base; a short secondary cable feeds the fitting. Primary = high voltage, long, in ducts; secondary = low voltage, a few metres, inside the base.
2

See it – a cut through the airfield

3

Size the isolating transformer

4

Lamp monitoring – three generations

5

Say this

“We supply the complete series circuit – 5 kV primary cable, moulded connector kits, isolating transformers sized for LED, light bases and smart LED fittings – so one supplier answers for the whole loop.”
6

Watch out

Most AGL failures are connectors and water in the light bases, not the lights. Use certified connector kits, tape and seal every joint, keep cans drained, and test the insulation of every circuit before energising. Only trained AGL electricians work on series circuits – the primary can be at several kilovolts.

PAPI – fly the glide slope

Move the aircraft above or below the 3° approach path and see what the pilot sees
1

In one line

The PAPI (precision approach path indicator) is four light units beside the runway. Each unit shows white above and red below its own angle. On the correct 3° path the pilot sees two white and two red.
2

See it

3

Watch out

PAPI units are aimed with an inclinometer to ± a few arc-minutes and checked by flight inspection. The setting depends on the glide slope and on the eye-to-wheel height of the largest aircraft (MEHT) – a PAPI set for small aircraft can bring a wide-body’s wheels too low over the threshold.

Constant current regulator & series circuit

One regulator drives up to a few hundred lights in series – change the step, the load and the regulator type, then break something
1

In one line

Airfield lights are not wired in parallel like buildings. They sit on a series loop fed by a constant current regulator (CCR) that keeps the same current (up to 6.6 A) through every light whatever the cable length or the number of lights. Each light hangs on an isolating transformer, so a failed lamp never opens the loop.
2

See it

Output current – one cycle

3

Say this

“A pure-sine-wave CCR handles LED loads cleanly – no flicker, low harmonics and accurate current at every step – and with full L-829 monitoring the tower sees load, insulation resistance and lamp failures for every circuit.”
4

Watch out

Series circuits run at several kilovolts on the primary – only trained AGL electricians work on them, with the CCR locked off. Size the CCR for the full load at 6.6 A plus cable and transformer losses, and check that the regulator is approved for LED loads (FAA AC 150/5345-10, IEC 61822).

Docking guidance & passenger boarding bridge

Dock an aircraft with the VDGS, then drive the boarding bridge to the door in 3D

In one line

An A-VDGS (advanced visual docking guidance system, e.g. ADB SAFEGATE Safedock) scans the incoming aircraft with a 3D laser, checks the type, and shows the pilot left/right and distance-to-stop guidance until STOP. In one line

A lighting management system switches and dims every light automatically – by time, by who is there (occupancy sensors) and by how much daylight comes in – and reports energy and failures.

In one line

KNX is the open world standard (ISO/IEC 14543) for building control – lights, blinds, heating, AC and security on one green bus cable. There is no central computer: every device has its own small brain and they talk with group addresses.
2

See it – press a button and follow the telegram

3

How KNX is built

Bus cableGreen twisted pair, 30 V DC safety extra-low voltage, 9600 bit/s (also KNX IP and KNX RF wireless)
LineUp to 64 devices on one power supply; more with line repeaters
TopologyLines → areas through couplers: 15 areas × 15 lines – tens of thousands of devices in one building
AddressesPhysical address (where the device is, e.g. 1.1.2) and group addresses (what it does, e.g. 1/0/1 “Office lights”)
ProgrammingOne tool for all brands: ETS, by a KNX-certified integrator
LightingSwitch / dimming actuators, or a KNX–DALI gateway to drive DALI drivers
4

Say this to the client

“KNX is an open standard with hundreds of manufacturers – you are never locked to one brand, and the same system runs your lights, blinds and AC. For the lighting itself we connect DALI drivers through a KNX–DALI gateway.”

Watch out

Open standards (KNX, DALI-2, BACnet, Modbus) let any qualified integrator maintain the system later. Proprietary systems (C-Bus, Lutron, Dynalite, Crestron…) can be excellent but tie the client to one brand and its partners for spares and changes – say so up front and price the service contract.
