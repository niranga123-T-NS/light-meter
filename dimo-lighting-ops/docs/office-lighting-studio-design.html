The practice project

Client brief: Harbour View Offices, 3rd floor

Every participant works on the same brief, so the instructor can compare designs and talk them through live.

  • Floor 20 × 12 m, clear ceiling height 2.80 m, 600 × 600 mm grid ceiling
  • Windows on the south and west façades
  • Open-plan office for 24 people, with screen work all day
  • Meeting room with a display screen and video calls
  • Manager's cabin, corridor and reception
  • Client asks: "Comfortable, no glare on screens, lowest energy bill, ready for smart controls"
The design process they practise

Nine steps, each one checked before moving on

Each step is a short explanation, one hands-on task on the plan, and an automatic check. A wrong answer is never just marked wrong. The check says what is off and why, for example "UGR 22: this panel is too bright for screen work, try a microprismatic optic". Participants can retry until the step passes.

  1. Read the brief and set the requirements

    TaskDrag the right EN 12464-1:2021 task onto each space: open office 500 lx, meeting room 500 lx, corridor 100 lx, reception 300 lx. Then set uniformity Uo, glare limit UGRL and colour rendering Ra. CheckEvery value matches the standard's table. The check explains that 500 lx is the minimum maintained value, not a target to beat by 50%.

  2. Room data and maintenance factor

    TaskEnter the reflectances (ceiling 0.7, walls 0.5, floor 0.2) and the 0.75 m working plane. Build the maintenance factor from LED life (L80 at 50,000 h), room cleanliness and the cleaning interval. CheckMF within ±0.03 of the model answer (about 0.80), with a slider showing how MF changes the luminaire count

  3. Choose the luminaires

    TaskPick from a deck of TRILUX office luminaire cards. Each card shows the photometry, efficacy (lm/W), UGR, CRI, mounting and optic. Choose a fitting for each space: recessed for the grid ceiling, suspended direct/indirect for open plan, downlights for the corridor. CheckThe mounting suits the ceiling, UGR meets the space's limit and the CRI is high enough. A wrong pick explains the reason, for example "an opal diffuser gives UGR > 19".

  4. Estimate the count: lumen method

    TaskWork out the room index and read the utilisation factor from the luminaire's UF table. Then solve N = E × A / (Φ × UF × MF) for the open office. CheckN within ±10% of the model answer. The working is shown line by line afterwards.

  5. Lay out on the plan

    TaskPlace the luminaires in rows and columns, snapped to the ceiling grid and lined up with the desks. A live false-colour map shows Em and Uo as they work. (Try the mock-up below.) CheckEm ≥ 500 lx and Uo ≥ 0.60 on the task area, spacing-to-height within the luminaire's limit, and no more than 15% over-lighting

  6. Glare, screens and comfort

    TaskRead UGR from the luminaire's UGR table for this room and viewing direction. Check the luminance limits above 65° for screens, then the walls, ceiling and cylindrical illuminance (Ez) for faces in video calls. CheckUGR ≤ 19, screen luminance limits met, wall, ceiling and Ez minimums met

  7. Controls and human-centric lighting

    TaskDraw the daylight zones along the façades and place presence sensors. Set absence mode, corridor linking and meeting room scenes. Choose whether to use tunable white for the open office. CheckEvery window zone has daylight dimming, every room has presence or absence control, and the meeting room has at least 3 scenes

  8. Energy and cost

    TaskCompare the design with the client's old T8 fluorescent fit-out: W/m², the LENI (EN 15193-1) annual energy per m² before and after controls, cost per year and payback. CheckWithin ±10%. The result feeds the client-facing summary.

  9. Design report

    TaskReview the report the app generates from their own design: luminaire schedule, plan, results per room, controls narrative and energy figures. Then send it to themselves. CheckAll rooms pass. This unlocks the challenge room and the certificate.

Live session

Two hours with a DIMO lighting engineer

Participants join on their own laptop or tablet with a session code. The instructor runs the session from the DIMO app and sees each person's progress live.

0:00Welcome: DIMO and TRILUX, what good office light does for people, and today's brief10 min
0:10Fundamentals, shown live: lux vs luminance, uniformity, glare (UGR), colour temperature, CRI, using interactive sliders15 min
0:25Guided practice, steps 1–5: requirements to layout, with the instructor demonstrating and the class following40 min
1:05Guided practice, steps 6–9: glare, controls, energy and the report25 min

Around the practice

What else the course includes

Instructor console

  • Start a session and share its code or QR
  • Live grid of participants and their current step
  • See who is stuck and on which check
  • Pause everyone, push a hint, show one design on the big screen

Scoring and certificate

  • Points per step, with fewer when hints are used
  • Challenge room scored separately
  • Pass mark 70%
  • Certificate: "Office Lighting Design – Practice", DIMO Dacademy, with the TRILUX product range named

For DIMO sales

  • Each client participant is recorded against their company in Customers or Consultants
  • Their report shows which TRILUX luminaires they chose
  • A follow-up task goes to the account owner

Self-paced mode

  • The same 9 steps without an instructor
  • Short explainer before each step
  • Progress saved, so they can stop and continue later
  • Available afterwards as a refresher
Access for clients

Clients join without an app account

Dacademy is open only to signed-in DIMO staff today. Clients need a lighter way in.

Instructor starts a session→Code and QR on screen→Client enters name, company and email→Practice in the browser→Report and certificate by email

Recommended A session code ends 7 days after the session. Clients can only see and save their own work, and the instructor sees the class.

Before building

Decisions needed

1. TRILUX product data and brand useWe need the official LDT files and data sheets for the luminaires in the deck, and TRILUX approval to use their name and logo in a client course. Which luminaire ranges should the deck include?
2. Client accessSession code with name, company and email (recommended), or full client accounts?
3. Live, self-paced or bothThe recommendation is both. The live session also works as a sales event, and self-paced mode serves as the refresher.
4. LevelThis design suits consultants and contractors. Should facility managers and developers get a lighter path that skips the lumen method and UGR tables?
